The Science of Chapter 2: Attention & Effort

Beatty & Kahneman's Pupillometry Discoveries (1966)

In Chapter 2 of Thinking, Fast and Slow, Daniel Kahneman recounts his foundational experiments conducted with Jackson Beatty. Using an infrared camera focused on participants' eyes, they discovered that the pupil serves as a precise index of mental energy allocation.

Key Principles Explained in the Book

  • The Electrical Meter of the Brain: Unlike heart rate or galvanic skin response, pupil dilation reflects the moment-to-moment rate at which mental energy is spent. Pupils expand as digits are held and computed, peak at the point of answer generation, and rapidly contract when the answer is voiced.
  • Working Memory Bottleneck: Humans can effortlessly retain 4 chunks. Transforming all 4 simultaneously demands continuous refresh cycles in the phonological loop and central executive.
  • The Law of Least Effort: System 2 is inherently lazy. Given alternative paths, the mind selects the least demanding. When forced into high load, peripheral awareness shrinks.
  • Attentional Blindness (The Invisible Gorilla): Intense System 2 concentration renders you functionally blind to peripheral stimuli. This app's optional probe demonstrates this effect directly.
